The short version

  1. Five crowd groups, one simple rule. A sports event, or a family show where most buyers are men: Family Night & Sports. Other family shows: Family Day. Rock or country concert: Bar. Pop concert: Pop. Everything else: Social.
  2. The groups make forecasts more accurate. Predicting each act from the other shows in its group cuts the error from 34% (no groups) to 22%.
  3. Each crowd buys differently. Bar crowds spend 7 times more on alcohol per fan than Family Day ($13.30 vs $1.80). Pop fans buy the most water, while hip-hop and comedy crowds lean on cocktails.
  4. Prices flex by group. Water $4.99 everywhere and family prices for both family groups. Adult crowds pay a little more for drinks: cocktails $30.25 and Michelob $17.49 at Pop and Social shows, and $30.99 and $17.99 at Bar shows.
  5. For the 9 upcoming shows: $1,055 more than at the old prices (+0.5%) and 4.0% more items sold, at our card and the base crowd. The card stays ahead of the old prices at every price sensitivity we tested, from 0x to 2x.

How the numbers are built

  • Units = crowd x units per ticket x (new price / price paid before)elasticity. Gross = units x price.
  • Last season at these prices takes each past show's real units and sales and re-prices them with the same formula, using what that show actually paid as the old price. It shows what our assumptions imply. It does not check them.
  • The real test is the backtest in the notebook: hide one act's shows, predict them from the other shows in the same group, and compare with what they actually sold. Error: 34% with no groups, 24% with our first 4 groups, 22% with the 5 groups (pop on its own).
  • Price sensitivity check: menu prices never changed in the data, so we can't measure sensitivity ourselves. We tried estimating it from the small differences in what fans paid within each group, and it was too noisy to use (95% interval from -1.84 to +3.97). The charts above re-run the forecast from 0x to 2x the published values instead.
  • Units per ticket come from the performer's own past shows when they played here before (P03, P06, P19, P33), otherwise from their group. E111 and E155 change between daytime and evening, so they get P25's daytime/evening ratios.
  • Crowd: base = Rule 2. High = the largest of Rules 1 to 3. Low = the smallest, except repeat acts get 75% of their past crowd, never below tickets already sold.
  • Elasticities are not in our data, so we use published averages: beer -0.46, spirits -0.80 (Wagenaar et al. 2009), soft drinks -0.79 and food away from home -0.81 (Andreyeva et al. 2010). The sensitivity switch scales all of them.
  • Groups: sports event or a family show where at least half the buyers are men = Family Night & Sports; other family shows = Family Day; rock or country concert = Bar; pop concert = Pop; everything else (hip-hop/R&B, comedy) = Social. Pop and Social keep the same prices; they are separate because pop crowds buy a different mix.
  • Concessions data covers most but not all points of sale, so treat totals as a consistent measure, not the arena's full revenue.
